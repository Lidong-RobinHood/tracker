# Translation glossary (binding for all catalogues)

Style: short UI wording as in mainstream fitness apps. German: informal "du", no "Sie"; no
gender-star forms; avoid long compounds where a short word exists. Chinese: Simplified, mainland
usage, no spaces between Chinese words, full-width punctuation (，。：？) with no space around it,
and one space between Chinese text and Latin letters or digits (`5 天前`, `已用 4 MB`, `你的 API 密钥`,
`80 kg`). No space inside dates (`11月18日`, as `Intl` writes them) or established compounds
(`T骨`, `维生素A`), and none around a placeholder whose value is Chinese text (`添加到{meal}`, `关于{title}`);
numeric placeholders get the space (`{count} 次训练`). Chinese: tap = 点按 (not 点击); one form for percent: `{n} %` with a space, as the app writes it; after a full-width
stop (。) no space before the next sentence. Units stay as they
are in every language: kcal, kg, g, ml, cm, %, h, min. Never translate: user data, brand and
product names, provider/model names, "Open Food Facts", "USDA", "YouTube", "API".

Catalogues already translated (app, settings, onboarding notices, common) take precedence where
they fixed a term not listed here; reuse their wording.

German only (de column): recovery range = Spanne (not Bereich); "log" = eintragen for one workout, set or diary
item, erfassen for tracking in general (food, weight); cable (equipment) = Kabelzug; split levels Einsteiger /
Mittelstufe / Fortgeschritten; record = Rekord (not Bestleistung); the four diary meals are neuter or masculine,
so sentences take the article: zum / beim / aus dem {meal}.
